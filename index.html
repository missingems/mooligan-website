Preface

Mooligan is a companion for Magic: The Gathering. It keeps the score at your table, holds a library of every card ever printed, reads cards through your phone’s camera, and follows what is winning in tournaments this week.

This book is its guide, in five parts, one for each colour of Magic. White, the colour of order, sits at the table and keeps the score. Blue, the colour of knowledge, keeps the library. Black, which knows the price of everything, keeps the ledger. Red, quick and impulsive, is the eye of the camera. Green, which watches what thrives, walks the field of the metagame.

Nothing here needs to be read in order. Turn to the part you came for, and let the index find the rest. Words with a dotted underline, like mana value, are in the glossary on p. 45.

Part One · White

1

The Table

Order, fairness, and the rules everyone at the table agrees to.

Chapter 1

Starting a Game

Open Play. Before anything else, Mooligan asks two questions: how many are at the table, and how much life each player begins with.

Twenty is the number for most formats. Commander begins at forty; choose it and every seat starts there.

Why forty? Commander games seat more players and run longer, so each player starts with twice the usual life.

Seats. Tap Start. The screen divides into one seat for each player, and each seat turns to face whoever sits behind it. Lay the phone flat in the middle of the table and everyone reads their own total the right way up.

Chapter 2

Keeping Score

Tap the upper half of your seat to gain a life, the lower half to lose one. Press and hold to count faster, which saves a good deal of tapping when something large connects.

The tally. Beside each total, a small tally keeps count of what has just changed. A hit for seven reads as −7 for a moment, so everyone can check the arithmetic before it settles into the total.

Useful when two things happen at once and someone asks, what was that?

Chapter 3

Ending a Game

When the game is decided, choose Rematch to reset every total and keep the same seats. It is the usual choice, since Magic is so often played best of three.

Choose New Game instead to start again with different players or a different starting life.

Part Two · Blue

9

The Library

Knowledge, patience, and the wish to know every card there is.

Chapter 4

Browsing

Every card belongs to a set, and sets are where browsing begins. They are listed newest first, each with its symbol and the date it was released.

Open one to see its cards in collector number order, the way they would sit in a binder.

Card to card. Open any card, then swipe sideways to reach its neighbour in the list you came from. A whole set can be walked this way, one card after another, as you would turn the pages of a book.

Chapter 5

Searching

Type part of a card’s name and results gather as you type. Search looks across every set at once, so a single name leads to every printing of that card.

When you know roughly what you want but not what it is called, filter instead.

Searching finds a card by what it is called. Filtering finds cards by what they are.

Chapter 6

Filtering

Filters narrow whatever list is in front of you: a set, or the results of a search. Choose by colour, by card type, by rarity, or by a range of mana value.

Filters stack. Each one you add narrows the list further, and each stays until you take it away.

By format. Choose a format (Standard, Pioneer, Modern, Legacy, Vintage, Pauper or Commander) to see only the cards legal in it. Combined with colour, it answers the kind of question deckbuilders ask: every blue instant legal in Modern, say.

Sorting, and starting over. Sort any list by name, mana value, price or release date. The filters in use sit in a row above the results; tap one to remove it, or Clear to begin again.

Chapter 7

The Card Page

Open a card and everything about it is on one page: the art, the rules text, whether it is legal in each format, and its rulings, newest first.

Tiles. Below the card, the page is laid out in tiles: legality, prices, pull rates, usage. Tap any tile to open it at full size, then swipe to move between them without returning to the card.

Double-faced cards. Some cards have two faces and no back. Turn a double-faced card over to read its other side; the rest of the page follows whichever face is showing.

Part Three · Black

23

The Ledger

Ambition, and the price of everything.

Chapter 8

Printings

Most cards have been printed more than once: in different sets, with different art, in different finishes. Printings lists every one.

Choose a printing and the whole page follows it: the art, the collector number, the price. It is the quickest way to find the version you own, or the one you want.

Chapter 9

Prices

Current prices are shown for each finish of the printing in front of you. Open a price to see its history.

History is what turns a number into a story. A card that has doubled overnight has spiked; one that has climbed for months is trending. The first often falls back. The second usually means the card is being played.

Prices are a guide, not an offer. They move daily, and differ from store to store.

Chapter 10

Pull Rates

How likely are you to open a card? Pull rates answer with the odds per pack, for each printing, in each product it comes in, and in each finish.

They are worked out from how each product’s boosters are put together. When a new set arrives, its odds follow once that make-up is known.

Part Four · Red

31

The Eye

Impulse, speed, and the moment in front of you.

Chapter 11

The Scanner

Point the camera at a card and hold it steady. Mooligan reads the card on the phone itself, and when it recognises the card, a copy floats up the screen.

For a clean read: good light and a flat card. Sleeves are fine; glare is not.

After a scan. Tap a scanned card to open it among its printings, showing the same face that was in front of the camera. From there it is an ordinary card page: turn it over, check its price, see where it is played.

Several in a row. Keep going. Cards can be scanned one after another without stopping, which is the quickest way through a stack of new pulls or a trade binder.

Part Five · Green

37

The Field

Growth, instinct, and what thrives.

Chapter 12

The Metagame

Choose a format to see what is being played in it. Archetypes, families of decks that share a plan, are ranked by their share of recent tournament results.

The field you see is this week’s, not last season’s. Watch it long enough and it grows and changes like anything living: one deck thrives, another adapts to beat it, and the balance moves.

Results are gathered from published tournaments, the numbers refreshed twice a day.

Chapter 13

Decklists and Usage

Open an archetype to see the decklists behind it, each with the event it came from and how it finished. Any card in a list opens its own page.

Usage. The same question, asked from the other direction. Open a card and see how widely it is played: its share of decks in each format and, for Commander, how often it is chosen across the whole format.

Chapter 14

Events

Magic is played in person, too. Find events at stores near you (Friday Night Magic, prereleases, Commander nights) and the premier play calendar of the larger tournaments ahead.

Store events come from Wizards’ store locator; the calendar from magic.gg.

Everything in this book is easier with someone across the table.

Glossary

archetype
A family of decks that share a plan and most of their cards.
booster
A sealed pack of cards. Magic sells several kinds, such as play and collector boosters, each put together differently.
collector number
The number printed on a card that fixes its place in its set.
Commander
A multiplayer format of hundred-card decks, one copy of each card, led by a legendary creature. Players begin at forty life.
double-faced card
A card with a face on each side and no ordinary back.
finish
How a printing is made: nonfoil, foil or etched, among others.
format
A set of rules for which cards may be played, such as Standard, Modern or Commander.
mana value
The total amount of mana in a card’s cost. Once called converted mana cost.
metagame
The decks being played in a format at a given time, and the balance between them.
mulligan
To shuffle away an opening hand and draw a new one, keeping one card fewer each time.
printing
One appearance of a card in one set, with its own art, number and finishes.
pull rate
The chance of opening a particular card in a particular booster.
rarity
Common, uncommon, rare or mythic rare, shown by the colour of a card’s set symbol.
rulings
Official clarifications of how a card works.
set
A release of cards sharing a name, a symbol and a short code.

Colophon

This book is set in New York, Apple’s serif, with running heads, notes and labels in SF Pro. Where those are not installed, Iowan Old Style or Georgia stands in. It is printed in one ink.
